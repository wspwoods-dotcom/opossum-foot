# Opossum Foot — A Trapper's Guide

A trap log built by a trapper, for trappers. If you can run a trap line, you
can run this app. This guide walks through everything one tap at a time.

## The idea in thirty seconds

You already know where your sets are. What you don't know — not really — is
what's *producing*. Which lure, which bait, which trap, which location is
actually putting fur up? Write it down every time in this app, and at the end
of the season it tells you. No more guessing in February.

## Putting it on your phone

**iPhone:**
1. Open the beta link in Safari (not Messenger, not Facebook — Safari).
2. Tap the Share button (the square with the arrow pointing up).
3. Scroll down and tap "Add to Home Screen."
4. Tap "Add." You'll see the Opossum Foot icon on your home screen now. Open
   the app from that icon from here on out.

**Android:**
1. Open the beta link in Chrome.
2. Tap the three-dot menu in the corner.
3. Tap "Add to Home screen" (or "Install app").
4. Open the app from the new icon.

**If a link comes to you in Messenger:** Messenger can't add things to your
home screen. Long-press the link, tap Copy, open Safari (or Chrome) yourself,
paste it in the address bar, and follow the steps above.

## The first time you open it

The app asks you to pick your state. Pick the state you trap in — the map
zooms right to it, and your season reminders come from that state's rules.
You can change it later in Settings.

Then it asks where your home ground is. Pick "Place it on the map" — pan to
your spot, tap to drop the pin, and drag it onto the exact place — or
"Use my GPS location" to set it where you're standing. Every time you open
the app, the map starts there. Each trap line gets its own home ground — your Iowa
line and your buddy's-farm line don't have to start in the same place. You
can change it any time in Settings.

## The tabs along the bottom

- **Map** — Your sets as pins on the map. Green: active. Amber: sprung.
  Gray: pulled. Blue: missing. Red means one thing: the set is past its
  trap-check interval (see below). Tap any pin to see that set.
- **Scorecard** — Which lure, bait, and urine is actually putting fur up,
  ranked by raw catch counts. Filter by season or species, or put two lures
  head-to-head.
- **Lines** — Your trap lines, each with its own mileage log.
- **History** — Every catch and event you've logged. (The report filters and
  CSV exports moved to Settings.)
- **Totals** — Your season numbers. Rows show the title and kept count
  only — tap one to see the details.
- **Seasons** — Season dates and reminders for your state, species by
  species.
- **Paperwork** — Your license photos plus the landowner notebook: names,
  phone numbers, permission slips, and boundary notes for every property
  you run.
- **Settings** — Tab toggles, feature toggles, set-field toggles, filters,
  backups, and the danger zone.

## Trap lines

A "line" is a group of sets you run together — say, your home line and the
line on your buddy's farm. Each line keeps its own sets, logs, seasons,
licenses, history, and totals, all separate.

- Only one line is active at a time. The brass-colored bar near the top of
  the screen always shows which line you're on — tap it to switch.
- To start a new line: go to the Lines tab and tap "+ Add a trap line." Give
  it a name and pick its state.
- When you start out, everything lives on Line 1. That's fine.

## Adding a set — step by step

1. Go to the **Map** tab.
2. Tap the **+** button (or the middle pin button to place the pin somewhere
   you're not standing: tap the map, drag the pin onto the exact spot, and
   hit "Looks right"). A form opens. While you're placing a pin, the rest
   of the app waits — finish the placement first.
3. **Name your set.** This one's required — the form names it for you
   ("Trap 1," then "Trap 2," and if you rename one "Creek crossing" the next
   becomes "Creek crossing 2"). Change it any time.
4. **Trap:** picking starts with the manufacturer — Bridger, Duke, Minnesota
   Brand, on down the list — then the type, then the size, so you land on
   the exact trap in three taps. Don't know the brand? Pick "Not sure /
   mixed brands" and you get the full unfiltered lists like before. Below
   that there's an optional Mods section — pan size, lamination, whatever
   you've done to the trap since it left the factory. It defaults to stock,
   so skip it if you run them as-bought.
5. **Snare purpose** (snares only) — Kill snare or Live-hold snare. Pick
   the honest one; it rides the set record and the exports.
6. **Bait, lure, urine, and other attractants** — what the set is carrying.
   Each gets its own stack of rows: bait, lure, urine, plus Other
   Attractants for the rest of it — feathers, squeakers, T-bones, foil,
   whatever you're running. Tap "+ Add Another" to stack more (up to six
   per field). Anything you type gets remembered, so next time you just tap
   it instead of typing it again. Fill these in honestly — this is what
   makes the Totals and Scorecard tabs worth anything.
7. **Set type** — Flat, Pocket, Dirt hole, and the rest, grouped Land and
   Water.
8. **Status** — four buttons now, not a dropdown: Active, Sprung, Pulled,
   Missing. New sets start Active. If a trap goes missing, the app asks
   why: Stolen, Mother Nature, Can't locate, Removed, or Confiscated (that
   one asks for the details and lets you snap the citation). Find it again
   and "Mark recovered" puts it back to Pulled.
9. Snap the set right when you make it — the form has a photo button now,
   so you can record exactly how you built it before you walk away.
10. Tap **Save set.** Your pin shows up on the map.

Running a line of identical sets? The form carries your whole rig forward
from the last set — same trap, same mods, same set type, same bait and lure
rows — so it's mostly tapping Save. Name, status, date, location, photos,
and notes never carry over; those are always fresh.

Changed something at a set — fresh bait, pulled the trap? Open the set from
the map pin and edit it. The app keeps the history straight.

## Logging a catch — step by step

1. Tap the set's pin on the map (or find it in History), then tap
   **"＋ Log a catch."**
2. The sheet opens with **Catch** and **Dispatched** already picked,
   because nine times out of ten that's what happened. The red asterisk
   marks what's required.
3. **Pick the animal.** The list runs A to Z with a search box at the top —
   type "coon" and Raccoon pops right up. If it was a neighbor's dog or a
   house cat, that's the "Domestic animal" row at the very bottom (tap the
   **?** next to it for what to do).
4. **Count** — how many.
5. **Disposition** — what happened to it:
   - **Dispatched** — it's going on the stretcher.
   - **Kept alive** — kept, but alive. Know your local rules on what you
     can do with a live animal.
   - **Released** — let go on the spot.
   - **Transported** — hauled out and released elsewhere.
6. Add a note if you want ("big boar, tore up the set"), or tap
   **🎤 Record memo** and talk instead of typing — the recording attaches
   to that catch, playable later from the set's memo list.
7. Save.

The app snapshots the set's bait, lure, and trap onto the log entry, so even
if you change the set later, the record of *that catch* stays honest.

**iPhone folks:** the Voice-to-notes button can't reach Apple's speech engine
in the installed app, so it'll tell you straight and point you at the fix —
tap the notes box, hit the mic key on your keyboard, and dictate. Same
field, same result.

## "Other events" — the stuff that isn't a catch

On the log screen there's a tab for other events. Use it for:

- **Sprung** — trap fired, nothing in it. (The disposition row hides itself
  for sprung traps — there's nothing to do with.)
- **Non-target animal** — caught something out of season or not a
  furbearer; the app won't count it as a catch.
- **Empty — nothing changed** — you checked the set and there was nothing
  to do: no catch, no change. The sheet strips down to date and notes —
  one tap and the check is on the record.
- **Fur** or **Animal part** — found sign worth recording.
- **Other** — anything else; describe it in the notes.

Logging sprung and empty traps matters — it's how the catch-per-night math
stays honest.

## Trap check reminders

Every set runs a check clock. The clock starts when the set is made and
restarts every time you touch it — an edit, a catch, an event, even an
Empty check-in. When an *active* set goes longer than your check interval
without any activity, its pin turns red on the map so the overdue ones
find you.

- Only active sets turn red. Sprung sets keep their amber pin — that
  already means "come reset me."
- The interval is fixed at 24 hours out of the box — that's the safe
  default anywhere. Where the rules allow longer, change it in
  **Settings → Trap Checks** (24, 36, 48, or 72 hours). The alerts can be
  turned off there too — the clock keeps stamping your visits underneath,
  so turning it back on doesn't paint the whole map red at once.
- Open the set to see the exact countdown: *Due in 6h* or *Overdue by 2d 4h*.
  Right below it is a **✓ Checked — nothing changed** button — one tap
  logs the visit, restarts the clock, and the pin goes back to green.
- Pulled sets don't get a clock — the trap's already out of the ground.
  Missing sets don't either — that's a recovery job, not a check rhythm.
- Old sets pick up the clock from the newest thing on record — when the
  set was made or its latest log entry, whichever is later.

## Totals — reading your season

Open the **Totals** tab. Everything here is for the current season, highest
to lowest.

- **By species:** what you caught, most to least.
- **By set:** what each set produced, most to least. Each row shows the
  title and kept count — tap it for the alive/released/transported
  breakdown plus the set's setup.
- **By disposition:** how it all shook out — dispatched, released,
  transported, kept alive.
- Toggle the sections on and off right on the tab.

## Scorecard — what's actually producing

The Scorecard answers one question: what's actually putting fur up? It ranks
your lures, baits, and urine by raw catch counts — not guesses, not
per-night math that falls apart when you switch lures mid-season. Every
catch gets stamped with what the set was carrying at that moment, so
changing a set later can't rewrite your past. Sets with nothing filled in
land in an "Unspecified" bucket at the bottom so the rankings stay honest.
Filter by this season, last season, or all-time; filter by species; or pick
two and run them head-to-head.

## Weather lives on the map now

There's no Weather tab anymore. The weather rides on the Map as a small
green chip on the left side, just above the layer button — condition icon
over the temperature.
Tap it and you get the full sheet: current conditions, wind, the next 12
hours, and the National Weather Service 7-day outlook with any hazard
alerts. Weather still auto-attaches to every new set and catch when you've
got a signal.

## Wind arrows on your pins

Zoom in close (14 or tighter) and every pin grows a little wind arrow. It
points where your scent is going — downwind, not where the wind's coming
from. The arrows refresh on their own; turn them off in Settings under
Feature Toggles if they're clutter.

## Follow-me GPS, how it actually works

One tap on the crosshair and the map follows you — and it stays following.
There's no off switch; dragging the map to look around pauses it ("Follow
paused — map dragged") and tapping the crosshair picks it back up. The +
button drops a pin at your live GPS spot right then and goes straight to
the New Set form. The middle pin button is for placing somewhere you're not
standing — tap the map, drag the pin onto the exact spot, hit "Looks right."
Pins never save on a stale location: if the GPS can't get a fresh fix, the
app says "Waiting for GPS…" and holds the save.

## Mileage

Every line has a Mileage button. Log trips with start and end odometer
readings — date, miles, notes — and the app keeps a running total. It also
draws a straight-line route estimate between your sets on the map (dashed
line; it reads 20–40% under real road miles, so treat it as a rough check,
not a receipt). Mileage exports to CSV with the rest.

## Bringing in Google Maps pins

Got pins in Google Maps? Export them (KML or GeoJSON) and import them in
Settings. Each pin becomes a set with its name carried over; you fill in the
trap and attractant details by tapping each one on the map. If a pin lands
on top of a set you already have, the app stops and asks — skip it, merge
it, or delete the incoming pin. Your app data is never touched by an
import.

## Saving photos to your phone

Every photo viewer in the app has a "Save to camera roll" button. It hands
the photo to your phone's share sheet — one more tap and it's in your
library. Handy for the license photos and the set-build shots.

## Deleting means gone

Delete means gone, everywhere. Deleting a set takes its pin, its catch logs
out of History, its photos, and its voice memos — the app warns you plainly
before it happens. You can also delete a single catch from History or from
the set's log list, same gone-forever warning.

## Seasons — the reminders

The **Seasons** tab lists your state's furbearers A to Z. Tap one for its
season dates, bag limits, and any notes. A badge shows whether it's open,
closed, or coming up.

Read the red line at the top of the tab: this is a **reminder, not legal
authority**. Seasons change, emergency orders happen, counties differ. Check
your state agency's current regulations before you trap — the app will never
tell you it's legal to set a trap.

## Paperwork

Open the **Paperwork** tab and tap "📷 Add license photo." Snap your license
(or your kid's, your buddy's — you can label whose is whose). It lives on
your phone so it's there when the warden asks, even with no signal.

The tab also holds the **landowner notebook**: names, phone numbers,
permission slips, and boundary notes for every property you run sets on.

## Backing up — read this one twice

There is no account and no cloud. Everything lives on your phone, which means
a lost phone, a wiped phone, or clearing your browser data can take your
whole season with it.

- In **Settings**, tap **"Back up all data (JSON)"** — that's the full
  backup: sets, catches, lines, landowners, the lot. Importing it restores
  everything. Do this one, not just the CSVs.
- **"⬇ Export catches (CSV)"** and **"⬇ Export sets (CSV)"** give you
  spreadsheet copies for Excel.
- Do it at least a few times a season. If the phone goes in the creek, the
  JSON file is your season.
- License photos and voice memos don't ride in any backup file — save the
  ones that matter to your camera roll.

## If something looks wrong

- **The app looks old or stuck after an update:** force-quit your browser
  completely (swipe it away) and reopen the app from the home-screen icon.
  Nine times out of ten, that fixes it.
- **Tabs or features went missing:** they're probably just toggled off.
  Check Settings — Tab Toggles and Feature Toggles are per trap line, so
  one line can run lean while another keeps everything.
- **Your location dot won't show:** check that you allowed location access
  for the browser, and that you're not in airplane mode.
- **Something's genuinely broken or confusing:** tell Tanner. That's what the
  beta is for — he'd rather hear about it than have you fight it.
